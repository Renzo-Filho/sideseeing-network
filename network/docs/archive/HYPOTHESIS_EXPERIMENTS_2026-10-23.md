# Sideseeing: testable graph hypotheses

Research design, 23 October 2026. Companion to [the research critique](RESEARCH_CRITIQUE_2026-10-23.md). These are proposed experiments, not results or changes to the current collection protocol. The West Loop cases below are *candidate cases* drawn from notebook diagnostics. They require video and independent physical verification before being used as reference examples.

## The scientific object

The unit we want to infer is a **physical pedestrian passage and its permitted transition to another passage**, not a smoothed walk trajectory. At an intersection, label specific approach and departure **ports**: for example, the west-side sidewalk approaching from the south, its curb interface, the crossing's near and far ends, and the receiving sidewalk. A line crossing on a map is neither an observed transition nor a graph node by itself.

Let `D` be timestamped GPS, IMU and video observations; `X` the unobserved collector trajectory and pose; `A` the association between observations and physical passages; and `G` a candidate passage graph with explicit transitions. A provisional joint formulation is `p(G, X, A | D, M)`, where `M` is an optional, **declared** map prior. The first experiments need not estimate this entire posterior. A bounded set of local alternatives with scores and consistency constraints is sufficient to ask whether uncertainty is useful.

Three input regimes should remain distinct: **street-only** (GPS/IMU/video, without OSM geometry or collection route at inference); **street plus aerial**; and **map-assisted** (OSM or planned route supplied as a prior). The present West Loop notebook is mainly a map-assisted diagnostic. It cannot supply a map-free baseline merely by hiding the final OSM layer, because OSM participates in fitting, side assignment and crossing matching. Spoken observations belong in a separately declared assisted condition or evaluation workflow.

## H1 — Can synchronized motion and video resolve the *right* graph ambiguity?

**Hypothesis.** Compared with a tuned GPS-only trajectory reconstruction, adding synchronized inertial events and video increases correct physical-side association and correct crossing attachment on held-out intersections. The strongest claim is about **topological decisions**, not a modest reduction in mean GPS coordinate error.

**Concrete candidate.** The notebook reports long stretches whose GPS fixes fall on the other half of the fitted street and highlights a possible Aberdeen/Madison detour toward May. Suppose the GPS trace appears to cross Aberdeen at one corner. Two hypotheses remain: the collector crossed there, or continued on Madison and crossed at May while the GPS drifted. Gyroscope changes can time a turn; video can show whether the collector entered a road crossing, continued along a curb, or passed a ramp. Neither alone proves the absolute geographic attachment. Independent field verification and reviewed video must establish the reference. The notebook's interpretation should not be inherited as ground truth.

A second, deliberately controlled case is a single block with sidewalks on both sides. Capture the same physical side in both walking directions, and both sides in the same direction where safe. This removes the current confounding of side with direction, lighting and what the camera faces. Include visually similar opposite sidewalks and a place with a near-parallel service path, because side classification on a simple isolated block is too easy.

**What to implement.** Fix a common candidate-generation budget for four conditions: (1) GPS-only, with trajectory smoothing and tuned passage clustering; (2) GPS + IMU event segmentation; (3) GPS + video event/scene cues; (4) all three. If a map prior is included, repeat *all four* as a separate map-assisted track, including a modern sidewalk-matching baseline. Use the same observations, spatial splits and candidate ports. A separate end-to-end comparison can then include any candidate-generation gains. IMU features should include turns and pauses, with sensor-to-camera alignment and timing quality recorded; video features should point to short supporting intervals, not only emit a class label. Do not infer an absolute side from relative yaw alone. A frame captured from one sidewalk may show the opposite sidewalk: distinguish camera trajectory from the location of the observed object.

**Reference.** At each held-out motif, two annotators independently identify physical passage IDs, approach/departure ports, transition time intervals and direct transition types. Adjudicate with a field revisit when recordings cannot settle the question. Label `unknown` when even that is unresolved. Keep reference annotators blind to model output. Video-derived reference is independent labeling, but not independent *sensing*; visibility limitations can affect both model and reference. Do not use voice or route plans to generate an automatic model label while claiming street-only inference.

**Primary outcomes.** Report side-ID accuracy and abstention, length of the longest continuous wrong-side assignment, crossing-event precision/recall under a predeclared time tolerance, exact receiving-port accuracy, false direct joins per intersection, and the fraction of crossing-sensitive OD queries for which a returned path is valid. Use site-level confidence intervals; repeated frames and passes over one connection are correlated. An ablation that improves event timing but not receiving-port identity is informative: motion resolved *when*, not *where*.

**What would falsify the useful claim?** Video may be unusable because of stabilization, occlusion, nighttime blur or clock drift. IMU may detect a corner turn while leaving the two candidate sidewalks equally plausible. A GPS-only method may already resolve all tested cases after fair tuning. If so, the paper should not market sensor fusion as a topology advance. It could still make a strong case for a specific assisted survey protocol or for uncertainty-aware routing under imperfect sensing.

**Publication boundary.** [Weng et al. (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43020-025-00159-8) already use a simple pedestrian map and smartphone sensors to identify the side of the street in urban canyons. Phone-sensor side positioning itself is therefore not the gap. The sharper question is whether side *and transition identity* can be recovered from sparse traversals when the pedestrian map is missing or itself uncertain, and how that affects routability. Be explicit that this is an inference from the compared task definitions, not a proven first-ever claim.

## H2 — Does retaining consistent alternative graphs improve route reliability?

**Hypothesis.** Given the same candidate passages, observations and scoring model, preserving a small set of mutually consistent local graph/association alternatives reduces invalid route claims at a **matched fraction of answered queries**, relative to early commitment and a tuned conservative single-graph baseline.

**Concrete case.** Imagine a crossing seen in video near an offset junction, but its far-side attachment is uncertain: `H_A` connects the observed crossing to sidewalk port `east-north`; `H_B` connects it to `east-south`. A second ambiguous trajectory is associated with one of those receiving passages. An indiscriminate union inserts both attachment alternatives, letting a router enter via one explanation and leave via another. That route has no coherent evidential story. An early maximum-score graph avoids the inconsistent union but may commit to the wrong attachment. A conservative graph can omit the crossing altogether. The proposed method retains `H_A` and `H_B`, and asks whether **one fixed proposed route** exists in a sufficiently supported, internally consistent set of hypotheses.

The Aberdeen/Madison/May diagnostic could instantiate this, but only after adjudication. Also construct a tiny synthetic motif to test the software logic: two mutually exclusive receiving ports and two routes that cannot coexist. This unit test is not empirical evidence for the paper; it protects the route evaluator from accidentally routing over a union of alternatives.

**Minimal representation.** Store immutable observation IDs; passage IDs and geometry; specific endpoint/port IDs; transition candidates; support and contradiction references with time intervals; unknown/occluded/coverage-boundary states; and local **exclusive-choice groups**. One observation may support several alternatives, but not be counted as independent evidence for both. Correlated GPS drift or repeated views from the same pass should have a shared dependency group. A small `k`-best list or a constraint-based enumeration over one or two intersections is enough for the first test. Do not claim calibrated probabilities until reliability checks support them.

**Baselines.** Compare (1) geometric union/snap of all candidates; (2) maximum-score single graph; (3) conservative single graph chosen by a threshold tuned on development data; and (4) retained consistent alternatives. Give each method the same candidate set and score features for the diagnostic comparison. Then compare complete pipelines separately, including aerial-only methods. Otherwise a gain in candidate recall could masquerade as better uncertainty handling.

**Route decision rule.** Define a query as start and end **physical ports**, not arbitrary points that might snap to the wrong sidewalk. Return one route with its support across complete graph hypotheses, a conditional route identifying unresolved transition(s), or `insufficient evidence`. A claim that no physical route exists needs a sufficiently complete reference; an unobserved continuation at the crop boundary is not a disconnected network. If heuristic ensemble weights are used, call the output a support score. Multiplying edge confidences independently is invalid when the edges share one uncertain association.

**Evaluation.** Preselect OD pairs: short crossing-sensitive pairs, same-side pairs, routes using two ambiguous junctions, verified disconnected pairs, and longer paths. At each answer-coverage level, measure the fraction of returned paths with a nonexistent passage or transition; separately measure reachable pairs missed, route stretch among valid routes, and abstentions. Report port-to-port reachability and direct-transition accuracy as well as PathwayBench-compatible metrics. A route can be topologically invalid despite plausible geometry or correct coarse boundary-to-boundary reachability. Cluster error intervals by physical site, since hundreds of OD pairs may share one faulty crossing.

**Critical control.** [Selective prediction](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v24/21-0048.html) and [probabilistic topological mapping](https://dellaert.github.io/files/Ranganathan10ijrr.pdf) are established. A system that answers nothing will have zero observed false routes but no utility. The meaningful result is a better risk–coverage curve than a well-tuned abstaining deterministic baseline, plus evidence that the alternatives explain particular failures. If that gain vanishes, a simpler conservative graph is the better method.

**Why publishable if it works?** [PathwayBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16875v1) makes routability central, while [TraversRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17479) advances aerial pathway graph generation. Sideseeing's potential addition is **observation-linked alternative physical-side/transition identities**, evaluated on real sparse ground-level capture and route decisions. The new contribution would be the specific inference/evaluation problem and empirical benefit, not the abstract existence of graphs or uncertainty.

## H3 — Can route impact tell us where to collect next?

**Hypothesis.** Given a fixed collector-time budget and a partially reconstructed graph, choosing revisits by their expected effect on uncertain route decisions resolves more verified OD queries per total collector-minute than revisit rules based only on geographic coverage, nearest distance, random choice or largest local uncertainty.

**Concrete choice.** Suppose one Morgan block has a wide lateral GPS interval: we do not know which side the track belongs to, but both candidate graphs still provide the same local OD routes. Five minutes spent there may improve coordinate/side labels without changing the selected routing benchmark. A nearby offset crossing may have a smaller uncertainty score but determine whether several predeclared trips require a long detour. A revisit that observes the curb-to-curb transition and far-side attachment might settle many route decisions. These are illustrative scenarios, not established facts about Morgan or May; actual travel/setup/review time could reverse the priority.

For an action `a`, predeclare a utility estimate such as

`E[reduction in invalid-or-unanswered benchmark queries after observing a] / E[walking + setup + recording + review minutes for a]`.

The numerator must average over **possible** revisit observations using a model learned on development sites. It may not read the held-out reference or future capture. Define the decision loss in advance, for example `L = c_false × invalid returned paths + c_abstain × unanswered queries + c_miss × known reachable pairs declared unreachable`, with `c_false` chosen to reflect the project’s routing stakes. The reference is used only afterward to evaluate the realized reduction in loss. Reducing abstentions by introducing wrong joins is not a success. Use a fixed OD distribution, including disconnected and crossing-sensitive pairs, so a policy cannot target only easy queries.

**Actions to compare.** Short reverse-direction pass on the same sidewalk; direct observation of a specific crossing and its receiving curb; a natural-walk pass; the current assisted spin/crossing routine; and a revisit under different visibility. Give each action a measured total time. Competing policies should include random feasible action, nearest unresolved site, unique-coverage first, maximum local uncertainty, and route-impact-per-minute. The candidate set must respect walking feasibility and collection safety; it should not contain a reference-only “perfect observation” action.

**First evaluation.** Offline replay is possible if later passes already exist: expose only the first day's streams, let every policy choose among actual later captures, reveal the chosen capture and update the graph. This gives a common test bed, but the available actions are biased by what collectors happened to record. Therefore interpret replay as feasibility evidence, not proof of optimal field planning. The stronger prospective experiment randomly allocates comparable ambiguous sites or collection blocks to policies, fixes equal time budgets, records actual travel and annotation effort, and evaluates on independently verified graph/route reference. Avoid training and testing on the same intersections. Stop or narrow the study if travel cost swamps any topology benefit.

**Scientific gap.** Information-driven exploration and active mapping already exist in robotics; see the [active-SLAM survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00254). The potential contribution is a **pedestrian-network-specific** value of information: which additional *walkable transition* should be observed, under realistic urban acquisition cost, to reduce harmful route claims. This deserves a separate paper only if it produces a repeatable gain beyond simple heuristics. Otherwise it is a useful internal field-planning rule.

## Cross-cutting aerial experiment

Aerial imagery is a candidate-generator and independent *viewpoint*, not independent proof of a connection. At a tree-covered corner, aerial imagery may suggest a sidewalk continuation while street video shows an occluded curb; the right state is unresolved. At a clear aerial gap with a street-level visible fence, evidence may contradict the continuation. At an untraversed mid-block passage, aerial may add a new hypothesis that a later walk verifies. Test aerial-only, street-only, naive union/snap and explicit correspondence with retained disagreement on the **same sites**. Stratify by occlusion, imagery date and whether ground capture traversed the segment. Report new true passages and false joins, not only pixel overlap. Keep the aerial imagery timestamp and licensing/provenance with each claim. Comparing with [Tile2Net](https://fmiranda.me/publications/pdfs/tile2net.pdf) or [TraversRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17479) requires their actual supported input conditions and, where possible, runnable implementations; otherwise label the substitute baseline honestly.

## A practical first study during West Loop collection

1. **Select and verify motifs.** Start with roughly 20–30 diverse corridor/intersection motifs if available: side ambiguity, offset crossing, nearby non-connection, known continuation past a recording boundary, occlusion, and simple controls. This is an ontology and effort pilot, not a powered benchmark. Treat Morgan and the current two Madison corners as development sites; freeze future nearby clusters as held out.
2. **Build the reference before tuning claims.** Double-label physical ports, side identity, positive and negative direct transitions, unknown states, coverage/visibility, and time-linked supporting intervals. Field-check irreducible ambiguities. Record adjudication time and disagreement. A voice note can trigger a check, but its absence is never a negative label.
3. **Prototype a small graph.** Reconstruct one block and one intersection as immutable observation records plus physical passages, ports, transitions and alternative groups. Run route queries through each consistent graph, never a union. Export verified deterministic portions to an [OpenSidewalks-compatible](https://github.com/OpenSidewalks/OpenSidewalks-Schema) graph while keeping unresolved alternatives in the research format.
4. **Run fair ablations.** Implement the four H1 modality conditions, the four H2 graph-decision conditions, and an aerial-only candidate condition. Freeze site-level splits and thresholds. Include a map-assisted track as an explicitly different experiment. Evaluate direct transitions and route decisions before polishing a citywide display.
5. **Use observed variance to size the study.** Predeclare a primary H2 endpoint, likely invalid returned routes at matched query coverage on held-out sites. Estimate effect and site-level variance from the pilot, then determine sample size and diversity for a final benchmark. One physical intersection, not one video frame or OD pair, is the principal independent unit. Only after this should a prospective H3 revisit allocation be powered.

Three decision gates keep the project honest. If synchronization/visibility cannot support event references, repair the acquisition protocol before claiming sensor fusion. If consistent alternatives do not beat a tuned conservative graph, simplify the inference contribution and emphasize the dataset/benchmark. If route-impact collection does not beat cheap heuristics per actual minute, do not make it the lead paper claim.

The strongest integrated paper would report a new benchmark of **evidence-linked pedestrian connectivity under sparse multimodal observation**, a bounded graph inference method, and a measured routing risk–coverage benefit. The benchmark paper can stand on its own if it exposes failure modes missing from existing graph benchmarks and includes rigorous baselines. H3 is a plausible second contribution, contingent on prospective evidence rather than an appealing planning story.
