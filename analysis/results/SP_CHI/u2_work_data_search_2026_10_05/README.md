> **Verification note (5 October 2026, `analysis/scripts/audit_u2_od2023.py`):** the source files this memo inspected had not been saved in the project; they were re-downloaded with hashes (see [u2_source_audit](../u2_source_audit_2026_10_05/README.md)). Confirmed: OD 2023 Table 14 totals (4,989,267 / 925,554 / 590,577), 343 zones, Brás 77,307, the residence-zone footnote, the city series totals and the Brás values, CMAP counts (7,310 / 2,825 / 1,871). **Corrected:** the 2022 city "invalid + not located" sum is **46,402** (10,381 + 36,021), not 65,558; the "invalid" row has a blank 2018 cell, and 29,537 is the 2023 value.

This is a source investigation, not a U2 score or a decision to change its estimand. The question is whether public data can locate work more faithfully in São Paulo's 96 districts and Chicago's 77 community areas than the current RAIS 2022 CEP and LODES 2022 workplace-block inputs.

## Most useful finding: São Paulo OD 2023 publishes jobs by work zone

The [Metrô OD 2023 archive](https://transparencia.metrosp.com.br/sites/default/files/Site_190225_PesquisaOD2023.zip) contains `Tabelas_Site_OD2023_REV_190225.xlsx`, its microdata and layout, and a 527-zone shapefile with `NumeroMuni`, `NumDistrit`, and `NomeDistri`. [Survey page](https://www.metro.sp.gov.br/pesquisa-od/).

- Table 11: estimated **jobs by employment zone and sector**.
- Table 12: jobs by activity class, including public administration, health, and education.
- Table 13: jobs by employment relationship, including formal employees, employees without a signed work card, public employees, self-employed workers, and other categories. This is a direct way to inspect the work that RAIS misses.
- **Table 14: jobs by location type**: fixed address outside residence; at residence; no fixed address. Its footnote says a no-fixed-address worker's employment zone was assigned to the **residence zone**. Thus `Total de Empregos` should not be mapped as all destination jobs. For a destination-worksite candidate, use `Empregos com Endereço Fixo — Fora da Residência`, and report home-work and no-fixed counts separately.
- Table 15: work performed externally (street/client visits) versus internally.
- The table note specifies employment zones 1–527 and first/second jobs; it estimates **jobs**, not only trips or unique workers. The table is from a household sample, so district estimates need precision checks. A usual workplace for a hybrid worker is also not a count of people present there on an average day.

I inspected the archive and joined Table 14's 527 rows to the supplied zone shapefile. `NumeroMuni = 36` identifies 343 zones and all 96 São Paulo districts. Summing the published weighted estimates over these zones gives **4,989,267 fixed-address jobs outside home**, **925,554 home jobs**, and **590,577 jobs with no fixed address** (the three components sum to **6,505,398**). These are exploratory source totals, rounded to jobs; no U2 density or score was constructed. As a spot check, the Brás district has 77,307 fixed-address jobs outside home in the OD table. The public microdata has 79,331 sampled people regionwide (`F_PESS = 1`); my first-job screening found only 4–1,709 fixed-outside-home observations per São Paulo district, with 9/96 districts below 50. This is a sample-size warning, not a calculated confidence interval.

## São Paulo administrative and sector sources

The city's [Infocidade work tables](https://prefeitura.sp.gov.br/web/licenciamento/w/desenvolvimento_urbano/dados_estatisticos/info_cidade/trabalho/260362) publish district RAIS employment by year and sector through 2024. The [2011–2024 district series](https://prefeitura.sp.gov.br/documents/d/licenciamento/2026-06-08_b_empregos_2011_2024) reports 5,390,446 jobs for 2022, 5,138,980 for 2023, and 5,346,517 for 2024, including unlocated categories. It defines invalid records as missing/invalid addresses and `Não Localizados` as records coded to the municipality whose address is outside it. The 2022 invalid plus nonlocated categories sum to 65,558, versus 508,844 jobs unlocated by the project's **CEP-only** allocation. This difference is a lead to investigate the city's geocoding method, not evidence that all of those jobs have a valid physical worksite.

**Year/location anomaly:** that same official series puts Brás at 420,022 jobs in 2022, 76,383 in 2023, and 36,733 in 2024. The OD 2023 estimate of fixed worksites there is 77,307. The very large 2022 administrative spike requires investigation before replacing the project data with the city district table. No cause has been established.

The [MTE RAIS microdata portal](https://www.gov.br/trabalho-e-emprego/pt-br/assuntos/estatisticas-trabalho/microdados-rais-e-caged) and the project's existing RAIS establishment query could support an establishment/industry investigation: the current CEP aggregate discards those details. The [CNES public health data API](https://apidadosabertos.saude.gov.br/) supplies establishment and professional data, a promising **sector-specific check** for hospital/clinic worksite locations. CNES professionals cannot simply be added to RAIS, because the same formal workers can appear in both datasets.

## Chicago sources

- **Current administrative baseline:** [2023 Illinois LODES 8 workplace area characteristics](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/lodes/LODES8/il/wac/) are available by 2020 workplace census block. I downloaded and opened `il_wac_S000_JT00_2023.csv.gz`; it has the expected `w_geocode` and `C000` columns. `JT00` counts all covered jobs and `JT01` primary jobs ([technical document](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/doc/help/onthemap/LODESTechDoc.pdf)). This is the direct update to the project's 2022 baseline. It does **not** add uncovered self-employment. [Census documentation](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/doc/pa-od-crosstate-flows-2002-2015.pdf) acknowledges employer workplace-address and multi-site reporting errors, so block precision should not be confused with ground truth.
- **Independent agency tabulation:** Illinois IDES [Where Workers Work](https://ides.illinois.gov/resources/labor-market-information/where-workers-work.html) provides annual March private-sector employment by Chicago ZIP and industry through 2025. It is based on unemployment-insurance records and explicitly warns that employers need not report by individual worksite; use it as a matched private-sector, coarser-geography check, not as a replacement for 77 community areas or an independent survey.
- **Recent worker-reported survey:** CMAP's [My Daily Travel Phase One, September 2024–June 2025](https://cmap.illinois.gov/data/transportation/travel-survey/) has public-use microdata and documentation. I downloaded the [public archive](https://stargishub01.blob.core.windows.net/cmap-arcgis-hub01-blob/Open_Data/TravelSurveys_PublicData_CMAP.zip) and inspected `person.csv`: it records primary/secondary work tracts, remote/hybrid status, and survey weights. It has 7,310 sampled people regionwide; 2,825 have a primary work tract and 1,871 of those tracts are in **Cook County**, an upper bound for Chicago city. This is useful validation at grouped geography, but much too thin to assume precise estimates for all 77 community areas without an uncertainty analysis.
- **Older household-survey option:** [2017–2021 CTPP workplace tables](https://ctppdata.transportation.org/CTPP_DataPortal_UserGuide.pdf) reach census tracts. The [methodology report](https://ctppdata.transportation.org/CTPP_Methodology_Report.pdf) says roughly 20–25% of workplaces are missing at detailed geography and that all tables use confidentiality-treated synthetic records. It also spans the pandemic. Investigate as a secondary check, not a straightforward new primary source.
- **Derived and coarser products:** [CMAP community snapshots](https://cmap.illinois.gov/data/community-data-snapshots/) contain useful employment summaries, but their local job-location estimates cite LODES/IDES; they are not independent worksite observations. [ACS workplace tables](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT1Y2024.B08604) and [BEA local employment](https://www.bea.gov/data/employment/employment-county-metro-and-other-areas) help with city/county totals, not the 77-area distribution. [ZIP Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html) is another ZIP-scale business check.

## What to test before choosing a U2 source

1. State the target: registered **job positions at employer addresses**, usual **fixed workplaces** including informal work, or **people physically working there on a typical weekday**. These have different denominators and cannot be treated as interchangeable. Current U2 proposes registered jobs per km² of land; the OD fixed-worksite column is a plausible alternative estimand, not a drop-in update.
2. For São Paulo, compare the district distributions of CEP-RAIS 2022, Infocidade district RAIS 2023/2024, and OD 2023 Table 14 fixed-outside-home jobs. Check raw sample sizes, uncertainty, and the Brás anomaly before using survey or city RAIS estimates as primary. The OD table allows sector and employment-relationship diagnostics that the earlier **work-trip** table could not.
3. For Chicago, process 2023 LODES under the existing block-to-area allocation, inspect `JT00` versus `JT01`, validate its private segment against matched-year IDES ZIP data, and compare broad work-location patterns with CMAP's weighted work-tract survey. Check remote/hybrid definitions.
4. Do not add overlapping registers and surveys to make a larger total. There is no verified comprehensive public source here for all workers **physically present at each neighborhood workplace on an average weekday** in both cities. Survey-informed correction is possible only after the above precision and universe checks.

The major official/public avenues searched were Metrô OD, São Paulo Infocidade/RAIS, MTE microdata, CNES, Census LODES/ACS/CTPP/CBP, Illinois IDES QCEW, CMAP travel surveys and snapshots, Chicago open business data, and BEA local employment. No dataset has been promoted into the U2 score by this search.
