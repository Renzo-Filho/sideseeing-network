# Chicago physical block construction and validation protocol (v1)

**Status:** pilot protocol, updated 26 September 2026. **Scope:** Chicago-only M3 block size and M4 block shape across the 77 Community Areas. **Current release decision:** M3/M4 remain unaccepted. The [complete-zone iteration](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_m3_complete_zone_pilot_v1_2026_09_26/README.md) develops an ordinary-street candidate and demonstrates why motorway deletion and imagery-only references are not yet reliable.

## 1. Research object and exclusions

A **ground-level physical street block** is a maximal connected land polygon separated from adjacent land by mapped, qualifying at-grade public street corridors. Its edge is the landward boundary of the street corridor, not a centerline buffer. A mapped alley internal to that land does not divide it; the block enclosure includes the alley, while net land excluding alley pavement can be reported separately. A cul-de-sac is evaluated by its actual corridor topology: if land remains connected around its end, it is one block; if the corridor joins another boundary and cuts the land, it may create two. Parks bounded by streets are eligible. The definition applies to unbuilt as well as built blocks.

Every resulting polygon also needs a *perimeter context*: ordinary street, motorway/ramp, rail, water, private street, airport operating surface, administrative clip, or unresolved. This avoids silently calling a motorway median or airport taxiway island a street block. Polygons bounded partly by rail, water, private circulation, or the airport perimeter are **special-context candidates**, retained for separate adjudication rather than silently included in the primary M3/M4 population. A freeway landscape island, roadway median, water polygon, airport operating/open land, or tile-edge clipping artifact is not a physical street block under this contract.

This is a **physical morphology** measure. Cook PIN groups, DuPage Parcel Blocks, Census blocks and cadastral lots are corroborating layers, not substitutes for a physical block. A high parcel overlap does not prove blockhood: nine visibly false freeway islands in the pilot each had 95.6%–99.2% Cook parcel-group coverage. Two distinct Loop blocks shared one seven-digit PIN group. See the [candidate audit](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_m3_candidate_v1_2026_09_26/README.md).

## 3. Candidate production: ordered procedure

**A. Freeze the run.** Record source snapshots, code commit, CRS, Chicago boundary, road/ROW code filters, alley semantics, small-artifact threshold, motorway rule and output schema. The present **3 m mapped-alley reopening** was selected in a small development sample before one ordinary-grid holdout. The 1,000 m² tile threshold used in earlier motorway stress scripts is only a pilot inventory threshold. A genuine smaller block must not be deleted solely because it falls below it.

**B. Build a continuous land surface.** Process the city in overlapping work tiles with a halo wider than expected blocks; produce polygons in the halo and stitch across seams before assigning stable IDs. Generate geometry globally before clipping for maps or Community Area summaries. Water and explicitly excluded operating surfaces are separate masks. Record all area lost to road, water, excluded land and geometry repair. Never call a clipped tile component a complete block.

**C. Construct street barriers.** Combine qualifying Cook ROW with ordinary Road Edge at the property side. Reconnect mapped interior alleys only where the mapped alley would otherwise split contiguous land. Preserve frontage roads and ordinary through streets. Do not turn every centerline into a fixed-width buffer. Check grade-separated crossings in ground plane before they are allowed to divide land beneath. The road mask and alley operation must be visually audited in pilot zones before reuse citywide.

**D. Generate all land faces.** Difference the land surface by the street barrier, make geometries valid, explode multipart results, and stitch tile seams. Give every face a candidate ID and retain even small or odd-shaped faces for review. Record gross enclosure area, net ground-land area after internal water exclusions, exterior perimeter, hole area, compactness `4πA_outer/P_exterior²`, elongation, boundary provenance, tile-edge status and source lineage. Internal water holes should not create extra exterior edges in M4. The earlier pilot's `compactness` field used a generic geometry perimeter; it is diagnostic and must be replaced by this frozen definition before M4 release. Check polygon validity, internal overlap, slivers, holes, and conservation of land area. Freeze coordinate precision and overlay operation order; test that algebraically equivalent corridor unions do not change face connectivity. The West Veterans Place diagnostic found a merge caused by a mask difference below 10⁻⁸ m².

**E. Detect missing boundaries before filtering.** Overlay municipal, Overture and OSM ordinary-street lines on each face. A named through street crossing a candidate interior without a corresponding ROW/Road Edge corridor is a **gap alert**. Use parcels and imagery to locate the possible landward edges; confirm road status, width and connection from a recorded plat, road-edge source or orthophoto trace. Store the evidence and resulting split geometry. If the edge cannot be established, keep a `merge_unresolved` candidate; deleting it would hide a missed block. West Veterans Place is the pilot fixture for this step.

**F. Apply explicit non-block flags.** Water overlap, pavement/ROW containment, motorway/ramp exposure, rail/airport operating context and tile-edge closure are *reason-coded flags*. The pilot motorway hypothesis is `area(candidate ∩ buffer(Overture motorway, 40 m)) / area(candidate) ≥ 0.30`. It is a review trigger, not a certified citywide deletion rule: the complete CHI:11 motorway core exposed a genuine residential frontage wedge with 0.674 exposure. Buildings and parcels cannot serve as mandatory positive tests because parks and vacant true blocks exist, while freeway islands can have parcels and some 100%-motorway-exposed faces contain mapped buildings. Keep each flag's source geometry, numeric value and rule version.

**F2. Add 3D physical-context evidence.** Co-register the 2022 DTM, DSM, orthophoto, road edges and candidate polygons; compare vertical profiles at elevated crossings and `DSM−DTM` support within suspect faces. Use classified LAS building/vegetation/road/bridge points only after verifying the delivered classes and their source dependencies. Keep no-data, temporal mismatch and resampling flags. The single-zone raster pilot separated five built faces from most, but not all, motorway slivers: two slivers had height fractions comparable to true blocks. A height threshold is a review signal, not an automatic acceptance/rejection rule or a legal boundary.

The [classified-point follow-up](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_m3_lidar_point_pilot_2026_09_26/README.md) extracted two specific 2022 Cook LAS tiles. In the first, two built faces have 29–30% class-6 building returns; two misleading height-rich freeway slivers have zero building returns and 44–57% vegetation returns. In the second, class-11 road returns trace the known West Veterans Place mask gap. A separate class-17 cluster is about 6.38 m above local ground. These are valuable **positive evidence and review triggers**. The same pilot found a county-mask-covered control with nearly no class-11 returns, so an absent class cannot automatically reject a street. The delivered metadata's detailed class table mislabels class 6 and omits class 5; verify the ASPRS mapping and spatial labels before freezing a classifier.

**G. Adjudicate into five states.** Each candidate becomes `accepted_core_block`, `accepted_special_context`, `rejected_nonblock`, `needs_boundary_repair`, or `unresolved`. Store a reason code, reviewer, timestamp, and evidence links. A rule can promote a flag to automatic rejection only after independent validation shows acceptable false-removal risk in every relevant context. Preserve rejected geometries and the full queue for audit.

## 4. Small pilot, iteration and independent validation

The current development sample consists of 12 fixed tiles across six Community Areas plus 26 older challenge polygons. The geographically new motorway check has four CHI:49 centers. These are **method-development evidence**, not representative Chicago performance. The [pilot assessment](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_block_protocol_pilot_v1_2026_09_26/README.md) records the candidate gates; the [established-method comparison](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_block_method_comparison_v1_2026_09_26/README.md) tests centerline polygonization and the published face-artifact rule on these selected cases.

Next, create an **independent reference inventory**, not a set of selected example polygons. Choose pilot zones *before viewing candidate outputs*, stratifying ordinary grid, Loop, parks, industrial/private access, motorway adjacency/interchange, rail, water, and Cook/DuPage O'Hare. Use zones large enough to contain complete blocks plus a margin; trace **every** qualifying block and mark every ambiguous perimeter. Keep reviewer sketches, source-date notes and exclusion points/polygons. A second reviewer should adjudicate ambiguous road status and splits. If only one reviewer is available, state that limitation and do not use candidate-exposed revisions as blind validation. Freeze reference files and hashes before running the candidate version being evaluated.

Match candidate and reference polygons one-to-one by maximum total IoU (with a predeclared matching cutoff), then separately report unmatched candidates, unmatched references, one-to-many splits, and many-to-one merges. A centerline gap cannot be evaluated by a false-positive filter metric alone. Report:

| Question | Measure |
|---|---|
| Are true blocks found? | Block-level recall and missed-block count, including merges. |
| Are proposed blocks real? | Candidate precision and false-positive count by reason. |
| Are outlines correct? | IoU, relative area error, median and 95th-percentile boundary displacement; show distributions, not only medians. |
| Are M3/M4 stable? | Area distribution and compactness/elongation error against reference, by context and by Community Area. |
| Do decisions vary by place? | Per-stratum metrics, ambiguous-share, source coverage, and confidence intervals clustered by zone. |

Compare at least three frozen ablations on the *same* independent reference: (1) road-centerline enclosure baseline, including an off-the-shelf implementation such as [momepy.enclosures](https://docs.momepy.org/stable/api/momepy.enclosures.html); (2) ROW/Road Edge with alley reconnection; (3) the proposed gap repairs and context filters. Include source-matched runs where possible (the same centerlines through alternative face-building implementations, then the same implementation with alternative boundary sources) so differences in inputs are not misattributed to the algorithm. Test the published [shape-based face-artifact heuristic](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/319) as an artifact-filter comparator if its inputs and implementation can be reproduced. Treat the [global multi-level block data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04704-7) as an external geometry comparison, not automatically as physical property-side truth. Report gains and losses, including real blocks removed by a filter. Changes after holdout inspection create a new version and need another holdout. Do not tune and report validation on the same tiles.

The pilot release gate is intentionally strict: no known systematic merge at a named ordinary street, no accepted freeway/airport/water non-block in the audited reference, and no unquantified exclusion of real blocks near motorways. Before citywide M3/M4 acceptance, freeze numerical floors for precision, recall and geometry/shape error **against the independent reference**, taking measured tracing disagreement and the Chicago model's district-level sensitivity into account. The current sketches cannot support defensible numerical accuracy thresholds or citywide precision/recall estimates.

## 5. Citywide execution and M3/M4 contract

**Production-first decision, 27 September 2026:** Generate the complete *provisional* 2D face layer now, then review and repair individual cases. This changes the order of work, not the accuracy standard for release. Keep every face, including tiny and odd-shaped polygons, and do not feed the provisional table into the model. The first citywide batch is documented in the [provisional-face run](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_m3_provisional_faces_2026_09_27/README.md). The [storage audit](DATA_SOURCES.md) sets an 8 GiB free-space floor and a 3 GiB pilot-output cap; raw LiDAR and imagery are acquired only for flagged cases.

After provisional generation, sweep the *entire city* for missing street divisions as well as false closed faces. Record every repair as a versioned operation against immutable input candidate IDs, retain rejected geometry, and distinguish proposed from adjudicated repairs. Then hold out geographically separated zones from every major context for final independent audit. Run conservation/overlap/topology QA, source-coverage maps, unresolved-case maps, and district-level stability checks. Perform a bounded manual audit of large, tiny, elongated and source-disagreement outliers in every Community Area. The citywide inventory should keep accepted, rejected and unresolved counts and area by context; a low-coverage Community Area cannot be silently scored as if it had complete blocks.

Assign a globally generated whole block to Community Areas by geometric intersection **after** its shape is measured. The proposed primary M3 area is its full *outer street-boundary enclosure* (including internal alleys and any internal water hole), because the research object is block scale; report net land area excluding internal water as a companion and sensitivity. M4 uses the same outer ring for perimeter and shape, with hole area reported separately. These definitions must be checked against the reference and frozen in the Chicago feature contract. For an area-weighted district distribution, use overlap area divided by full block area as its district weight and record boundary-crossing share. Do not clip the block first and then calculate M4 shape, because the administrative edge is not a physical block edge. Freeze the M3 distribution statistic(s), M4 shape statistic(s), inclusion of special-context blocks, missingness policy and district weights before fitting the Chicago-only model. M4 is eligible only when its underlying M3 block set is accepted.

The deliverables for each run are: source manifest and hashes; rule/config version; full candidate geometry table; boundary/flag provenance; repair ledger; adjudication queue; independent reference inventory; matched comparison table; per-context and per-area QA report; citywide accepted block table; and a model-facing M3/M4 table with coverage and uncertainty fields.

## 7. Novelty assessment

The basic operation is **not novel**. [Long and Liu (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6165) derived urban parcels from OSM road networks at large scale. [Muhs, Meinel and Burghardt (2013)](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2013/_extendedAbstract/304_proceeding.pdf) described automated urban-block delineation, gap closure and artifact handling. [momepy](https://docs.momepy.org/stable/api/momepy.generate_blocks.html) already generates blocks from street networks and morphological tessellation. [Fleischmann and Vybornova (2024)](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/319) specifically proposed a shape/area heuristic to detect false urban-block faces in street networks and tested it across 131 cities. A [multi-level street-block dataset](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04704-7) also uses road classes and buffers at global scale. These works rule out a claim that road-derived polygons, artifact filtering, or centerline-buffered blocks were invented here.

### Use established methods first

We are using the established **generate faces from road barriers, inspect artifacts, measure shape** framework. The [direct comparison](../../analysis/results/Chicago/chicago_block_method_comparison_v1_2026_09_26/README.md) found that `momepy.enclosures` and direct Shapely polygonization give identical faces on the same municipal linework in all tested halos. The published face-artifact index also produces a Chicago threshold, but does not flag every known non-block control. We should reuse the established implementation where it fits and benchmark each custom source repair or context rule against it. `momepy.enclosures` takes line barriers, so municipal-centerline enclosures remain subject to road-source omissions and centerline rather than property-side boundaries. `momepy.generate_blocks` uses building tessellation and assumes building-linked cells; it is a different target for parks and unbuilt land. The global road-buffer dataset is a useful external comparison but does not certify Chicago property-side edges. These limitations explain why an off-the-shelf output is a baseline rather than the final answer to this particular physical-boundary contract.

**Encountering a problem is not itself a research contribution.** It becomes evidence only when the failure is defined, counted on a complete independent reference, explained by source or topology, and a reproducible fix improves the result without creating worse errors elsewhere. The current pilot establishes concrete examples and a direct baseline, but it does not meet that standard yet.

### Future possibility, conditional on successful validation

> A potential contribution is the tested combination of property-side boundaries, source-gap repair, ground-level topology, context-aware filtering and explicit uncertainty. That claim would require a complete independent reference, comparison with existing methods, and preferably testing beyond Chicago. It is not established by this pilot.

If the plan succeeds, the defensible outputs could be: **(1)** a source-traceable Chicago physical-block polygon dataset with quantified coverage and error, if no equivalent validated property-side dataset already supplies the same object; **(2)** an independent Chicago benchmark containing complete true blocks, non-block artifacts and split/merge cases across contexts; **(3)** an empirical comparison showing when centerline, published artifact-filter and property-side methods succeed or fail; and **(4)** robust Chicago M3/M4 measurements and substantive findings from the Chicago-only model. Those are data, benchmark, empirical and application contributions even if the polygonization algorithm is established. A **method** contribution would additionally require an independently validated improvement from the combined repair/filter workflow over the baselines, with ablation showing which steps cause the improvement. A general method claim would be stronger with a second city or substantially different source regime.

This is a focused prior-art check, not an exhaustive systematic literature review. It should be repeated before any paper or public novelty claim.
